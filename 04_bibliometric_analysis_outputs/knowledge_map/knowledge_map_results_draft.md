## Results


### 1. Perfil del corpus y crecimiento

The corpus comprises 96 articles published between 2014 and 2026, with a compound annual growth rate of 16.1%. The most productive year was 2025, with 12 publications, yet the recent five-year period (38 articles) represents a 13.6% decline compared to the preceding five years (44 articles), suggesting a recent deceleration in output. The corpus has accumulated 1,299 citations, with a mean of 13.53 citations per article and an h-index of 18. The citation half-life is approximately 9 years (median citation year 2017), and the Price Index median of 27.7% indicates that just over a quarter of cited references are less than five years old. The most cited work, with 296 citations, is the self-efficacy instrument by Mamaril et al. \cite{mamaril_measuring_2016}. The corpus draws on 320 authors, 47 journals, 34 countries, and 120 institutions. The top five journals cover 21.9% of the corpus, with the *International Journal of Engineering Education* as the core journal (7 articles). Metadata completeness averages 68.4% across eight fields, with notable gaps in funding information (13.5% of articles funded) and PubMed identifiers (2.1%). Social sciences is the dominant research domain (55 articles), and 55.2% of articles map to SDGs, primarily Quality Education (37 articles). As shown in Figure 5, the annual growth trajectory is uneven, with a clear peak in 2025 followed by a decline.


### 2. Estructura intelectual (co-citacion, espectroscopia)

The intellectual structure of the corpus, examined through co-citation spectroscopy, reveals a field whose conceptual foundations are anchored in psychological theory, particularly social cognitive frameworks. The most influential seminal works identified are Bandura’s publications from the 1970s, including his 1977 work on self-efficacy cited 42,860 times and his 1978 unifying theory of behavioral change cited 10,604 times, alongside an unidentified 1961 psychology reference with 7,010 citations. This strong grounding in psychological theory is consistent with the corpus’s emphasis on constructs such as self-efficacy and identity, as seen in recent validation studies \cite{power_engineering_2026,power_self-efficacy_2025,verdin_testing_2017}.

Concept lifecycle analysis shows that the core concepts of Psychology, Computer science, and Engineering education all reached their peak citation frequency around 2020, followed by a decline phase beginning in 2021. This synchronous pattern suggests a field that experienced a period of intense activity and is now consolidating. The concept of Engineering, with a total of 314 references and a peak of 37 annual citations in 2020, follows a similar trajectory, as does Engineering ethics (290 references, peak in 2020) and Medical education (364 references, peak in 2020). The co-citation network, illustrated in [Insert Figure near here: network_cocitation_cluster.png], visualizes the clustering of these influential references.

The Leiden analysis indicates that the corpus’s citation impact is slightly below the world average, with a Mean Normalized Citation Score (MNCS) of 0.927 and only 19 of 96 articles (19.79%) performing above the field mean. The proportion of papers in the top 10% most cited (PP(top 10%)) is 0.094. A notable anomaly is the 'Unknown' field, comprising six articles with an MNCS of 0.00, which, along with identified language, geographic, and document type biases, suggests that citation metrics should be interpreted with caution.


### 3. Estructura social (colaboracion, SCP/MCP)

Of the 96 articles in the corpus, 74 provided country affiliation data, enabling an analysis of international collaboration. Single-country publications (SCP) dominated, with 61 articles, while 13 involved multi-country collaboration (MCP), yielding an MCP rate of 17.57% [95% CI 9.46-27.03]. This pattern is illustrated in Figure [Insert Figure near here: Fig43], which visualizes the SCP/MCP distribution. The remaining 22 articles lacked country data and were excluded from this classification.

Despite the overall prevalence of domestic research, a subset of articles functioned as structural bridges between distinct research communities. Notably, \cite{mamaril_measuring_2016} connected four communities with an interdisciplinarity score of 0.76, while \cite{khampirat_validation_2021} and \cite{idkhan_employability_2021} each bridged four communities with scores of 0.75 and 0.71, respectively. More recent works, such as \cite{balgiu_validation_2024} and \cite{partanen_chemical_2024}, also bridged four communities, with interdisciplinarity scores of 0.56 and 0.83. These bridging articles, concentrated in instrument validation, suggest a connective role within the field's collaborative structure, as further depicted in the country collaboration network (Figure [Insert Figure near here: Fig28]).


### 4. Estructura conceptual (keywords, temas, clusters)

The conceptual structure of the corpus, derived from keyword co-occurrence, inductive thematic analysis, and network centrality, reveals a field organized around the psychometric validation of competency measurement instruments, with self-efficacy and professional skills as central constructs. Keyword frequency analysis [Insert Figure near here: keyword_frequency.png] and temporal trends [Insert Figure near here: keyword_trends.png] indicate that terms such as "self-efficacy," "motivation," and "professional competencies" dominate the literature, while the co-citation network [Insert Figure near here: network_cocitation_cluster.png] identifies two pivotal bridge documents: Bandura’s *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* (betweenness = 0.050) and a subsequent work by the same author (betweenness = 0.036), which connect four and three distinct research clusters, respectively. These nodes suggest that social cognitive theory serves as a foundational framework linking instrument development to broader educational outcomes.

Inductive thematic analysis, triangulated across multiple analytical streams, identified several high-robustness themes. The most prominent, "Self-Efficacy and Learning Experiences in Engineering Education" (approximately 30 studies, high robustness), is consistent with the centrality of self-efficacy measures in the corpus, as seen in instruments validated by \cite{mamaril_measuring_2016} and \cite{paul_examining_2018}. A second high-robustness theme, "Professional, Ethical, and Generic Competencies in Engineering Education" (approximately 22 studies), aligns with work on professional skill assessment \cite{motahhari-nejad_professional_2021,tormey_development_2023}. Other high-robustness themes include "Motivational and Career-Related Constructs" (approximately 9 studies), "Critical Thinking and Decision-Making" (approximately 6 studies), and "Impact of Experiential Learning" (approximately 8 studies). Themes with medium robustness, such as "Socio-Emotional and Psychological Competencies" (approximately 3 studies) and "Sustainability and Ethics Competencies" (approximately 2 studies), suggest emerging but less consolidated lines of inquiry.


### 5. Frentes de investigacion y topicos emergentes

The analysis of concept lifecycles and research frontiers reveals a field in a phase of consolidation rather than rapid transformation. Among the 25 top concepts examined, the majority are classified as foundational (22 concepts), with only three identified as active, and none declining. This distribution suggests that the conceptual architecture of engineering competency measurement is relatively stable, with current work building upon established constructs rather than introducing entirely new ones. The research frontiers identified through burst detection point to specific areas of methodological and thematic momentum. The term ‘structural equation modeling’ appears as a new growth frontier, reflecting the field’s increasing reliance on advanced psychometric techniques for instrument validation, a trend evident in recent corpus work \cite{garriott_development_2021,gercek_engineering_2023,khampirat_validation_2021}. Similarly, ‘self-efficacy’ shows sustained growth (growth ratio 1.65×, with 11 recent occurrences), and ‘quantitative’ methods display a growth ratio of 1.68×. These patterns are consistent with the inductive theme ‘Self-Efficacy and Learning Experiences in Engineering Education’, which emerged with high robustness and encompasses approximately 30 studies. The theme ‘Professional, Ethical, and Generic Competencies in Engineering Education’ (approximately 22 studies, high robustness) and ‘Motivational and Career-Related Constructs in Engineering Education’ (approximately 9 studies, high robustness) further indicate that the field’s primary focus remains on psychometrically sound measurement of learner attributes. The presence of pivot nodes, such as Bandura’s foundational work on self-efficacy (betweenness = 0.0355, connecting three clusters), underscores the theoretical anchoring of these measurement efforts. The topic evolution, illustrated in [Insert Figure near here: topic_evolution.png], visualizes the trajectory from broad competency frameworks toward specialized instrument validation.


### 6. Vacios y direcciones futuras

The corpus reveals several structural and explicitly declared gaps that delineate promising directions for future inquiry. A triangulated analysis of declared research needs identifies the development of engineering identity in non-engineering contexts as a salient lacuna, with authors noting that identity development has been studied with non-engineering populations \cite{choe_quantitative_2017}. Similarly, the application of community-partnered projects (CPPs) in capstone courses is well established, yet how specific instructional designs enhance competencies remains underexplored \cite{kittur_examining_2024}. A further declared gap concerns the limited examination of systems thinking surveys specifically among engineering students \cite{huang_systems_2015}, and the interplay between grit and engineering mindset on academic achievement has only recently been addressed \cite{nandy_dual_2025}.

Structural gap analysis between methodological approaches and application domains reinforces these observations. Several method–application pairs exhibit zero co-occurrence in the corpus, suggesting systematic under-exploration. Regression analysis remains unconnected to medical education, pedagogy, medicine, and physics, while case study approaches are absent from medical education and medicine. Experimental designs are similarly disconnected from physics. These structural voids, each with a priority score exceeding 300, point to methodological opportunities that have not yet been pursued within the measurement literature.

The typical study in this corpus draws on a median sample of 344.0 participants (mean = 721.1; range 50–6078 across 56 studies), indicating that while large-scale validation efforts exist, many instruments have been tested on relatively modest samples. Future work could benefit from cross-validation across larger and more diverse populations, particularly in the unexplored methodological–application intersections identified here.


### 7. Indicadores de impacto y calidad

The corpus exhibits a notably high Field-Weighted Citation Impact (FWCI), with a mean of 3.812 [95% CI 2.31-5.77] and a median of 2.149 [95% CI 0.68-3.10], indicating that over half of the articles (57.10% [95% CI 42.86-71.43]) exceed the world average. This elevated impact is concentrated rather than uniform: the proportion of articles in the top 10% of their field (PP(top 10%)) is 9.38% [95% CI 4.17-15.62], corresponding to nine articles, while a bootstrap cross-check of the Mean Normalized Citation Score (MNCS) yields 0.989 [95% CI 0.62-1.48]. Citation trajectories, classified by the Citation Success Symbol system, show that 72 articles are poorly cited, 16 fairly cited, 7 remarkably cited, and 1 outstandingly cited. A small subset of articles displays a delayed-recognition pattern, with the most prominent sleeping beauty (NLXE3X6S, B=20.58) peaking in 2024 after nine dormant years \cite{huang_systems_2015}. The median Price Index of 27.7% suggests a moderate recency of cited references. The APC-paid articles (n=10) show a mean of 35.68 citations [95% CI 13.37-69.89] versus 8.06 [95% CI 5.43-11.18] for non-APC articles, but the Mann-Whitney p-value of 0.015 and a confidence interval crossing zero indicate this gap is not statistically distinguishable, warranting exploratory interpretation only.


### 8. Patrones transversales (hallazgos triangulados)

The triangulation of findings across analytical streams reveals a coherent thematic architecture centered on measurement development and validation within engineering education. The most robust convergence, supported by cross-model validation between two independent LLM families (Jaccard similarity of 0.29 for theme names), identifies three strongly convergent themes: "Motivation and Engagement in Engineering Education" (combined similarity 0.323), "Self-Efficacy and Engineering Identity Measurement" (combined similarity 0.317), and "Instrument Development and Psychometric Validation in Engineering Education" (combined similarity 0.312). These themes align closely with the inductive analysis, where "Self-Efficacy and Learning Experiences in Engineering Education" (approximately 30 studies, high robustness) and "Motivational and Career-Related Constructs in Engineering Education" (approximately 9 studies, high robustness) emerge as dominant empirical clusters, consistent with foundational corpus contributions such as the measurement of engineering self-efficacy \cite{mamaril_measuring_2016} and the validation of contextual competence scales \cite{ro_validity_2015}.

A secondary layer of triangulated themes includes "Professional, Ethical, and Generic Competencies in Engineering Education" (approximately 22 studies, high robustness) and "Critical Thinking and Decision-Making in Engineering" (approximately 6 studies, high robustness), reflecting sustained attention to transversal skill assessment. The bibliographic coupling network [Insert Figure near here: network_bibliographic_coupling_cluster.png] illustrates the structural connections among these thematic clusters. Meanwhile, themes with lower study counts—such as "Socio-Emotional and Psychological Competencies" (approximately 3 studies, medium robustness) and "Representational Competence in Engineering" (approximately 2 studies, medium robustness)—appear as suggestive but less consolidated fronts, warranting cautious interpretation given their smaller evidentiary base.


### 9. Dinamica investigativa 3D (Research Dynamics Manifold)

The Research Dynamics Maniford (RDM) maps the 96 corpus articles across three axes—Emergence, Impact, and Connectivity—to reveal the field’s structural and dynamic character. The dominant axis across the corpus is Impact (33 of 96 articles, 34%), closely followed by Connectivity (32) and Emergence (31), indicating a balanced but slightly citation-driven research landscape. The 96 articles distribute into eight archetypes, with Silent Connectors being the most frequent (21 articles, 22%). These works are characterized by high structural importance but low citation impact and low novelty, suggesting they function as methodological or bridging contributions that are undervalued by conventional citation metrics. For instance, the validation of a motivation and self-efficacy questionnaire \cite{segarra_understanding_2026} (S=0.77, I=0.23) and a confirmatory factor analysis of instruments \cite{kotla_wip_2025} (S=0.72, I=0.23) exemplify this archetype. At the other end of the spectrum, Paradigm Shifters (18 articles, 19%) exhibit high performance across all three axes. The top-ranked article by RDM score (0.804) is the measurement of undergraduate engineering self-efficacy \cite{mamaril_measuring_2016}, which combines high emergence (E=0.69), impact (I=0.84), and connectivity (S=0.87). A second Paradigm Shifter, assessing motivation for sustainable engineering \cite{mccormick_assessing_2015} (RDM=0.738), and a third, the contextual competence scale \cite{ro_validity_2015} (RDM=0.736), further consolidate this pattern. Eight articles are classified as Isolated Seeds, showing high mean emergence (E=0.59) but low impact and connectivity, representing niche innovations not yet integrated into the field’s core structure.


### Synthesis

The field is characterized by a strong, sustained focus on psychometric instrument development and validation, particularly for self-efficacy and motivation constructs, which serve as a methodological backbone connecting theoretical foundations (Bandura), empirical studies, and emerging areas like socio-emotional competencies. The convergence of multiple phases on self-efficacy measurement indicates a mature subfield, while the emergence of compassion and identity measures suggests a broadening of the research agenda toward holistic engineering education.
